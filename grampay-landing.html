01

What it actually does

Off-ramping crypto to Naira usually means leaving the conversation, opening an exchange, and walking a withdrawal form by hand. GramPay collapses that into a tool call your agent makes directly — the connector pattern used to let AI move money, aimed at one job: getting crypto into a Nigerian bank account.

01

Quote & balance

The agent reads your USDC balance and a live USDC → Naira rate before proposing an amount.

02

Prepare, then execute

A prepare step validates limits and issues a signed one-time token; execution redeems it. Nothing moves without both halves.

03

Settle to a bank account

Naira lands in a named Nigerian bank account through IvoryPay — and the agent can track the reference until it clears.

→

One conversation, four moves

The full cash-out runs as a short, inspectable sequence. The prepare/execute split is the safety seam — a signed token has to change hands before any Naira does.

Safeguards

Built to be careful with money

Signed one-time tokens

prepare issues a JWT that expires in 5 minutes; execute must redeem it. Prepared transfers survive restarts and scale without a database.

Hard cashout cap

Every payout is bounded by GRAMPAY_MAX_CASHOUT_USD — $1,000 by default. Amounts over the cap are refused before anything is signed.

Real banks only

An anti-wallet filter keeps payouts pointed at licensed Nigerian bank accounts, never at arbitrary crypto addresses.

Two transports, one auth

Streamable HTTP for remote connectors, stdio for local desktop. Both gated by a Bearer token you control.

Roadmap

Where it stands, plainly

Operating boundaries, today

  • Single operator. One fund owner today; the multi-user approval layer is on the roadmap, not shipped.
  • IvoryPay is the live rail. Validated end-to-end; Breet and Quidax open after incorporation.
  • Hard $1,000 cap per cashout while volume ramps — raised deliberately, not automatically.
